\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Multi-Sensor Fusion for 3D Object Detection}
\label{subsec:21}

Multi-sensor detectors combine observations through spatial correspondence and feature interaction. BEVFusion~\citep{liu2023bevfusion} brings camera and LiDAR features into a unified bird's-eye-view (BEV) space, while TransFusion~\citep{bai2022transfusion} and CMT~\citep{yan2023cmt} establish cross-modal interactions through LiDAR-guided queries and 3D positional encoding, respectively. For radar--camera fusion, RCBEVDet~\citep{lin2024rcbevdet} combines radar BEV encoding with deformable cross-attention for feature alignment. These designs offer different ways to establish correspondence and integrate sensor features.

Recent studies further address modality contributions and varying input quality. MoME~\citep{park2025mome} routes queries among camera, LiDAR, and joint expert decoders. CCF~\citep{wu2026ccf} combines separate query supervision, geometric priors, and complementary masking to address modality imbalance in cross-domain detection. RobuRCDet~\citep{yue2025roburcdet} uses Gaussian expansion of radar points and camera-confidence-guided fusion to accommodate different sensor disturbances.

On K-Radar, 3D-LRF~\citep{chae2024threedlrf} and L4DR~\citep{huang2025l4dr} investigate spatial interaction, foreground denoising, and gated fusion. DLRFusion~\citep{chae2025dlrfusion} separately encodes Doppler, radar power, and LiDAR features for iterative interaction. WCBR~\citep{li2026wcbr} adjusts branch contributions according to environmental conditions, while SRF~\citep{mukhatbekov2026srf} fuses stereo images and radar in 3D voxel space. For three-sensor fusion, ASF~\citep{paek2025asf} uses unified canonical projection and cross-sensor attention within corresponding patches, whereas RAF~\citep{park2026raf} modulates camera features using explicitly supervised image reliability maps. Building on ASF's projection and local interaction mechanisms, ObjDec makes shared and modality-specific representation decoupling an explicit part of the local fusion architecture.

\subsection{Shared and Modality-Specific Representation Learning}
\label{subsec:22}

Explicitly organizing shared and specific information has an established foundation. Domain Separation Networks~\citep{bousmalis2016dsn} distinguish shared and domain-specific representations for domain adaptation. MISA~\citep{hazarika2020misa} learns modality-invariant and modality-specific representations through similarity, difference, and reconstruction constraints for affect analysis. FactorCL~\citep{liang2023factorcl} retains task-relevant shared and unique information, while DeCUR~\citep{wang2024decur} learns common and unique representations through cross-modal and intra-modal correlation objectives. These studies support learning consistency while preserving useful differences.

Recent work further explores how these representations are selected, aligned, and used. \citet{liu2025featurecausality} use a causal formulation to further separate modality-specific information into unique and redundant components. MultiLoReFT~\citep{tonekaboni2026multiloreft} learns shared and modality-specific subspaces through low-rank representation fine-tuning. DecAlign~\citep{qian2026decalign} uses prototype-guided optimal transport and distribution matching for modality-specific and common representations, respectively. For detection, SPFD~\citep{wang2026spfd} separates RGB--event shared and private features through frequency-domain coherence and uses them in both its encoder and decoder. LMD~\citep{park2025lmd} instead provides post-hoc decomposition of modality contributions in pretrained fusion networks.

Building on this line of work, ObjDec uses shared and modality-specific representation decoupling to organize local fusion for multi-sensor 3D detection. Its design connects spatially corresponding sensor patches, representation constraints supervised within object regions, and direct use of the decoupled representations in token updates and cross-modal interaction. Object guidance gives the learning and use of these representations a detection-specific focus, while representation decoupling provides the organizing principle of the fusion architecture.
